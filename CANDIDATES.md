# Candidate Defences — Five Seconds to Explain the Glass

Competition: u-po-pu-five-seconds-to-explain-the-glass-934d9949
Brief: one sentence, at most 200 characters, the excuse a house cat gives
in the five seconds after knocking a glass off the table, in the cat's own
voice. No apology and no confession — the most convincing case a cat can
make for itself. Judge (GLM-5.3-flash, comedy-editor persona) compares two
entries at a time and picks the one that would most convince it the cat had
a real reason, weighing in order:

1. one sentence of at most 200 characters in which a house cat excuses
   knocking a glass off the table
2. sounds like a cat's own logic, self-interest and total lack of remorse,
   rather than a person apologising
3. lands a fresh comic idea rather than the obvious gravity, slippery-edge
   or it-was-already-falling lines
4. fits the five seconds: a spoken line a cat could plausibly deliver, not
   a written paragraph of reasoning
5. uses every character well, with no padding, throat-clearing or repeated
   joke

Rules I applied: one sentence only (no second sentence hiding behind a
period), no apology ("sorry", "my fault", "I didn't mean to"), no
confession, no gravity / slippery-edge / already-falling lines, no
"a cat walks into a bar" shape, original wording throughout. Two live
entries already on the board were checked and deliberately not repeated:
"It was blocking my view of the bird..." and "The other cat in the glass
started it".

## Reasoning

The strongest three were #1, #3, and #18.

- #1 ("sunbeam ... mine by law") is the only candidate that scores full
  marks on every criterion. The motive is unmistakably a cat's — a
  sunbeam is sacred territory — and the defence is pure cat law: the glass
  was trespassing on property the cat already owned, so removing it was
  not an accident but an eviction. Zero remorse, zero apology, one breath,
  84 characters with nothing wasted. Nothing in it touches the judge's
  banned obvious lines (gravity, slippery edge, already falling).
- #3 ("the glass chose its fate") has the best single punchline of the
  set — the tail-spot is a real, specific cat grievance — but its comic
  idea is a shade less fresh than #1's property-law framing, and it
  explains slightly less of a *reason* for the knock.
- #18 ("only I am allowed to stare") is the tightest line at 74
  characters and the most speakable, but "the glass was staring at me"
  leans on the same mirror/reflection territory as a live entry already
  on the board, so it risks reading as a variation of a line the judge
  has already seen.
- #9 and #13 were scored down on freshness for the same reason: each
  closely echoes a live entry ("blocking my view of the bird", "the other
  cat in the glass started it"). Originality against the field matters
  when the judge compares entries two at a time.
- #23 is #1 with ", not yours" appended. The extra clause adds a sting
  but dilutes the snap: the shorter version lands the law joke and stops,
  which is exactly what a five-second line should do.

## Chosen entry

> That glass was standing in my sunbeam, and in this house the sunbeam is mine by law.
